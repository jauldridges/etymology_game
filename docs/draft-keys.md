# Draft Keys

Four Latin keys are drafted from memory and every entry is unreviewed. Each word must be checked against an etymological dictionary and approved by a person before its puzzle is published.

**Flags**

- English *mess* (from *missus*, a course sent to the table) and *Mass* are real but indirect, so they sit in the bonus tier.
- Spanish and Portuguese *meter* ("to put in") has drifted far in meaning but is a genuine descendant.

| English | bonus | legislate |

**Flags**

- Many English words come from the *lat-* stem (*relate, translate, elate*), which players will not expect from *ferre*. This makes a good reveal.
- *Tolerate* shares a root with *tulī* but does not descend from *ferre*; *extol* comes from the separate verb *tollere*. Both are left out.
- *Legislate* comes from *lex* plus *lātor* ("proposer"), so it is bonus-tier.
- Spanish *oblea* and French *oublie* ("wafer") come from *oblāta* ("offering").

## *dare* (dō, dare, dedī, datum): give

Stems: *da-*, *dat-*, *ded-*; compounds use *-dit-* and *-dere*. Languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese, French. About 120 entries; the compound tier may become its own puzzle.

| Language | Tier | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | core | data, datum, date (calendar date), dative, donate, donation, donor, pardon, condone, dowry, endow |
| English | compounds | add, addition, additive, edit, edition, editor, tradition, traitor, treason, betray, extradite, extradition, render, rendition, surrender, rent, vend, vendor, perdition, mandate, mandatory, command, commander, demand, commend, recommend |
| Spanish | core | dar, dato, datar, dado, dativo, dádiva, donar, donación, donante, perdonar, perdón, condonar, dote, dotar |
| Spanish | compounds | adición, editar, edición, editor, tradición, traidor, traición, extradición, rendir, renta, rendición, vender, venta, vendedor, perder, pérdida, perdición, esconder, mandar, mandato, comandar, demandar, recomendar, encomendar |
| Portuguese | core | dar, dado, data, dativo, dádiva, doar, doação, doador, perdoar, perdão, dote, dotar |
| Portuguese | compounds | adição, editar, edição, editor, tradição, traidor, trair, traição, extradição, render, renda, rendição, vender, venda, vendedor, perder, perda, perdição, esconder, mandar, mandato, comandar, demanda, recomendar, encomendar |
| French | core | donner, don, donation, donateur, donneur, donnée, date, datif, dé (die), pardonner, pardon, dot, doter, dotation |
| French | compounds | addition, éditer, édition, éditeur, tradition, traître, trahir, trahison, extrader, extradition, rendre, rente, rendu, reddition, vendre, vente, vendeur, perdre, perte, perdition, mandat, commander, commande, demander, demande, recommander |

**Flags**

- The compounds (*add, edit, tradition, render, vend, perdition*) look like *dare* compounds but come from a second Latin verb, *-dere* ("put"), that merged with *dare*. Many dictionaries file them under *dare* anyway. Whether they count as core is an open question.
- *Mandate, command, demand, commend, recommend* are *manus* + *dare* ("put in someone's hand").
- Lookalikes to exclude: English *dare* and *give* (Germanic); *date* the fruit (Greek); *dose, antidote, anecdote* (Greek *didōmi*, a cousin, not a descendant); Spanish *añadir*, which comes from *adnectere*, not *addere*.
- To verify: Spanish *dado* and French *dé* ("die") from *datum*, and English *betray* via *tradere*.

**Flags**

- *Melior* and *optimus* (the comparative and superlative of *bonus*) come from different roots, so *ameliorate, optimum, mejor, meilleur* do not belong here. They would make good puzzles of their own.

- Uncertain and left out: English *bonny* (probably from French *bon*) and *boon companion*.
- The *bene-* words are compounds with *facere*, *dicere* and *velle* (*benefit, benediction, benevolent*), so they could also appear in puzzles for those verbs.

## *caput* (caput, capitis): head

Stem: *capit-*. Languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese, French. About 120 entries, so the larger families may be trimmed to fit a puzzle of about 100.

| English | bonus | cattle, chattel, capitol, corporal (military rank) |
| Spanish | core | cabeza, cabezudo, cabecera, cabecilla, cabecear, encabezar, cabo, acabar, capataz, jefe, chef, capitán, capital, capitalismo, capitalista, capitalizar, capitel, capítulo, capitular, recapitular, decapitar, caudillo, cabildo, precipicio, precipitar, bíceps, tríceps, cuádriceps, occipital |
| Portuguese | core | cabeça, cabeçalho, cabeceira, cabecear, cabecilha, encabeçar, cabo, acabar, capataz, chefe, chef, capitão, capital, capitalismo, capitalista, capitalizar, capitel, capítulo, capitular, recapitular, decapitar, caudilho, precipício, precipitar, bíceps, tríceps, quadríceps, occipital |
| French | core | chef, chef-d'œuvre, chapitre, chapiteau, chevet, achever, achèvement, couvre-chef, capitaine, capital, capitale, capitalisme, capitaliste, capitulation, capituler, récapituler, décapiter, décapitation, cheptel, cadet, caboche, cap, caporal, précipice, précipiter, précipitation, biceps, triceps, quadriceps, occipital |

**Flags**

- *Capere* ("take": *capture, capable, accept, receive*) is a different root and the biggest trap in this family. None of those words belong here.
- English *cap* and *cape* (cloak) come from Late Latin *cappa*, whose origin is uncertain, so they are excluded. *Cape* (headland) does come from *caput*.
- *Caper* and *Capricorn* come from *caper* ("goat") and are excluded.
- *Precipice* and *precipitate* come from *praeceps* (*prae* + *caput*, "headfirst"). This surprises players and makes a good reveal.
- *Corporal* (rank) comes from Italian *caporale*, from *capo* ("head"); it is bonus-tier because it is easily confused with *corporal* (of the body). *Capitol* is bonus-tier because the origin of *Capitolium* is debated.
- Spanish *jefe* and Portuguese *chefe* came through French *chef*.
- To verify: *cabildo, caudillo* (from *capitellum*), *capataz, caboche, cheptel*, and English *cadet* and *caddie*.

## *graphō* (γράφω): write

Stems: *graph-* and *gram-* (from *gramma*, "letter, something written"). Languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese, French. This family is very large, about 200 entries across the four languages. A puzzle uses about 100, so the technical tier is the first to be cut.

**Flags**

- *Gram* (the unit) comes from Greek *gramma* by way of Late Latin, where it meant "a small weight." *Kilogram* is built on it.
- *Program* is *pro-* + *gramma* ("written beforehand"). *Cartography* is a hybrid of Latin *carta* and Greek.
- *Glamour* is a Scottish alteration of *grammar*, and *grimoire* is French for the same word. *Graft* and French *greffe* come from *graphium* ("stylus"). All are bonus-tier because the link is indirect.
- English *carve* is a distant cousin through Proto-Indo-European, not a descendant, and is excluded. *Grave, engrave, grab* and *grape* are unrelated.
- To verify: *glamour, grimoire, graft/greffe*, and the Spanish and Portuguese *grafiti* and *grafite*, which share spellings with *graffiti* and *graphite*.

## *hūs* (Old English *hūs*): house

Germanic cousins puzzle. Written forms: Old English *hūs*, Old Norse *hús*, Old High German *hūs*, and Gothic *-hūs* (in *gudhūs*, "temple"). The reveal may show the reconstructed Proto-Germanic ancestor \*hūsą as extra context. Languages: English, German, Dutch, Swedish. About 85 entries.

| Language | Tier | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | core | house, housing, household, housewife, housework, housekeeper, houseboat, housefly, warehouse, courthouse, farmhouse, doghouse, greenhouse, lighthouse, outhouse, hothouse, workhouse, madhouse, slaughterhouse, townhouse, clubhouse, treehouse, playhouse, birdhouse, husband, hussy, hustings |
| German | core | Haus, Häuschen, häuslich, Hausfrau, Hausmann, Haushalt, Hausaufgabe, Hausarzt, Hausmeister, Haustier, Haustür, Rathaus, Krankenhaus, Gasthaus, Warenhaus, Kaufhaus, Bauernhaus, Treibhaus, Gewächshaus, Gehäuse, hausen |
| Dutch | core | huis, huisje, huiselijk, huisvrouw, huisman, huishouden, huiswerk, huisarts, huisdier, ziekenhuis, stadhuis, gasthuis, warenhuis, huisvesten, behuizing |
| Swedish | core | hus, husdjur, hushåll, sjukhus, stadshus, bostadshus, husvagn, husmor, husrum, husarrest |

**Flags**

- This is a cousins puzzle. *House, Haus, huis* and *hus* share an ancestor; none comes from another. *Husband* and *hustings* are different: they come directly from Old Norse *húsbóndi* ("master of the house") and *húsþing* ("house assembly"). *Hussy* is a shortening of *housewife*.
- Germanic languages build compounds freely, so the family is unlimited. The key lists only compounds found in a standard learner's dictionary.
- Distant cousins left out: *hut*, *hide* (conceal) and *hood* may share a far older root with *house*, but the link lies beyond the written evidence. The reveal can mention it. *Hose* and German *Hose* ("trousers") are a separate word.
- Danish and Norwegian *hus* are also cousins, but are not in this puzzle's language set.
- The German, Dutch and Swedish entries need a check by a fluent reader before use.
